Mitigation:
- End-to-end encryption: TLS/SSL, SSH, and IPSec encrypt the payload and authenticate segments, making sequence guessing useless.
- Random initial sequence numbers (ISN): Modern OS implementations use cryptographically secure random ISNs (RFC 6528) to prevent sequence prediction.
- Network security: Dynamic ARP inspection, DHCP snooping, and 802.1X prevent ARP spoofing and in-path positioning on the local network.

3. TCP RST Attack
A TCP RST attack is an attack where an attacker abruptly terminates an active TCP connection by injecting a forged TCP packet with the RST (Reset) flag set.
In normal TCP operation, the RST flag is used to cleanly abort an abnormal connection (e.g. port closed or host rebooted). In an attack, it is abused to disconnect legitimate sessions.
How the attack works:
1. Sniffing or Guessing: The attacker identifies the 4-tuple (source IP, source port, destination IP, destination port) of an active connection.
2. Sequence Number Matching: The attacker crafts a TCP packet with the RST flag set. The sequence number in the RST packet must fall within the receiver's valid receive window.
3. Injection: The forged RST packet is transmitted to either endpoint (client or server).
4. Immediate Termination: Upon receiving a valid RST, the endpoint immediately tears down the connection and deletes its TCB.
